The research question

Remembering a fact is one thing.
Learning a working standard is another.

People teach AI through corrections, requests, and self-descriptions. These interactions reveal individual process preferences: standards for evidence, depth, analytical focus, procedure, and delivery.

ProcessPrefBench asks whether a preference expressed in one dialogue can shape a later answer when the new task does not repeat it.

Paper & code

A benchmark for cross-task transfer.

ProcessPrefBench: Evaluating Cross-Task Transfer of Individual Process Preferences from Naturalistic Human–AI Interaction

Each case pairs a known-relevant source dialogue with a later target task. Preference realization and severe task failures are judged separately. The benchmark tests application of a supplied preference source; it does not test retrieval from a long history.

The product behind the question

Meet Margin.

A place to read, annotate,
and think with AI.

While building Margin, a reading and annotation tool, we kept returning to a question: can an assistant learn how a person wants to work from their everyday interactions?

Those natural conversations became the starting point for ProcessPrefBench. Margin is the product context behind the research; this paper evaluates preference transfer under controlled prompting conditions.
